What should we do in that minute, when we are waiting for our food to warm up in the microwave, for the lift to arrive, or for a printer to print out some documents?

A minute feels excruciatingly long when we are idly waiting.

Before you reach out for your phone in your pockets, what if there were something else you could do instead, something that takes just a few seconds and leaves you feeling genuinely rested?

After all, *don’t we all deserve a break*?

---


As it turns out, the solution might just be closing our eyes for a bit.

Of sight, hearing, smell, taste, and touch, which would you be most willing to let go of, if you had to choose one? Only few amongst us would choose sight, because we live in a predominantly visual world, from paintings to billboards and from books to reels.

**Closing our eyes gives our brains and bodies a physical and mental break.**

When we close our eyes, our body enters a state often described as **quiet wakefulness**. The eye muscles that help us focus and look around relax. Our hearts beat at a lower and more flexible rhythm, reflecting a shift into a more restorative mode, while remaining highly responsive to unexpected demands.

Our brains also get a break from processing the constant stream of visual information. At the same time, we get a chance to attune to our internal bodily and mental signals, called interoception.

So not only is our cognitive load reduced, shutting our eyes frees up resources for memory consolidation and supports creativity and imagination. As a result, we get a genuine moment of rest while improving our clarity and mental balance in just a few seconds.

The best part of this? We do not need to buy an expensive gadget with a low battery life for this. We do not need to make time for this; *it literally takes a few seconds*.

---


### Remember
The next time you have a moment to spare, instead of reaching out to your phone, try closing your eyes for a brief moment.
